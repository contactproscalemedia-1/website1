# Room 01 — Executive Boardroom

## Creative Intent

The visitor should feel like they have entered the executive headquarters of ProScaleMEDIA.

The space communicates discipline, accountability, precision, and strategic decision-making.

Nothing feels decorative.
Everything feels intentional.

## Visual Composition

- Split layout.
- Left 40%.
- Right 60%.
- The left side contains editorial typography.
- The right side contains a real architectural executive boardroom.
- The typography always dominates.
- The photography supports the typography.

## Photography Direction

Use real editorial architectural photography whenever possible.

Never use obvious AI-generated scenes as final assets.

Preferred subjects:

- Executive boardroom
- Architectural concrete
- Dark oak
- Stone
- Glass
- Natural morning light
- Long conference table
- Museum-quality interior photography

Avoid:

- People
- Laptops as hero objects
- Handshakes
- Marketing clichés

## Human Presence

The room should feel occupied without showing people.

Allow subtle objects such as:

- Leather notebook
- Pen
- Glass of water
- One chair slightly pulled back

Nothing more.

## Lighting

- Single natural light source.
- Soft morning light.
- Tree shadows are acceptable.
- No glow.
- No bloom.
- No HDR.
- No dramatic volumetric effects.

## Materials

- Concrete
- Dark oak
- Black steel
- Smoked glass
- Leather
- Stone

Nothing glossy or futuristic.

## Dashboard

One vertical performance display.

Minimal information only.

Preferred metrics:

- Ad Spend
- ROAS
- Qualified Leads

Avoid:

- Decorative charts
- Fake interfaces

## Typography

- Headline occupies most of the left panel.
- Large.
- Editorial.
- Almost oversized.
- Supporting copy remains quiet.

## Negative Space

- Large breathing room.
- Never fill empty space just because it exists.

## Color

- Black
- White
- Muted warm gray
- Brand red only as an accent

## Mood References

- Apple
- Leica
- Porsche
- Wallpaper*
- Kinfolk
- Financial Times
- McKinsey

If the logo is removed, the room should still feel like a premium executive environment rather than an AI-generated luxury office.
